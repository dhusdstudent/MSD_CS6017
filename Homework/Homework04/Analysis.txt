##After finishing all of my tests, I've drawn the following conclusions:

##In my opinion, none of the models performed notably poorly - though none were perfect, either. While the margin is very thin, I think I'd have to estimate that the SVM model performed the best here. I mostly come to this conclusion because it definitively captured the true negatives and did at least manage one true positive. It stumbled a little when it came to false positives, but definitively had no false negatives. While it doesn't end up mattering too much in my particular dataset (being that misclassifying a wine's quality is relatively low stakes) there could be a real-world scenario where the priority of analyzing data could well be to not have any false negatives. None of the other models managed a 0 in any of the 'false' categories, so I would say that this one is preferable, but it really depends on your priority.

##I thought that the decision tree was perhaps a little difficult; even though as a model on its own, it's perhaps one of the more straightforward options. I think that this particular dataset didn't lend itself well to a branching interpretation, which is what made it more difficult. If I had to guess, I'd say it's because there are a lot of individual components at play in overall quality, which tripped up its predictive capabilities.

## I believe that, of the four models from my experiment, the best suited to scale would be Logistic Regression, and maybe SVMs to a lesser degree. These options are more efficient than KNN and Decision Trees, who generally don't handle large datasets all that well due to difficulties with storing and overfitting.

## I didn't face too many challenges with my particular dataset; largely because it was consistent and didn't have any values that needed to be replaced with dummy variables. It seemed to also provide a pretty consistent level of depth across the details being tracked, which I think lent itself to testing whether certain factors of a given wine can help us to predict whether it will have a good or bad quality.
